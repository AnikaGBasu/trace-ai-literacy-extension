# Practice task — map scale

Reference instrument • materials-1.0.0.

## Participant page

Your goal is to understand how a map scale connects a distance on a map to the real distance. Use the chat and notes workspace to prepare an explanation. This practice is for learning the interface.

You may begin with: **“Explain how to use a map on which 1 centimetre represents 2 kilometres. Include a simple example.”** You may change this prompt or ask follow-up questions.

1. Send a question in ChatGPT and read the response.
2. Make a note. Try selecting and copying one passage into the notes area.
3. Complete the practice reflection when it appears: “Explain the map scale in your own words and give one distance example.” You may also try **Skip for Now**. A skip does not prevent you from continuing.
4. Tell the researcher when you know how to use the chat, notes, reflection, and skip controls.

The researcher will now hide the chat and notes. Answer:

- Practice explanation: What does “1 cm represents 2 km” mean?
- Practice application: Two places are 3 cm apart on this map. What is their real distance? Explain your calculation.
- Practice claim check: “On this map, 3 cm represents 3 km.” Is this correct? Explain what you would check.
- Practice confidence: How confident are you that your explanation and application are correct? 1 Not at all; 2 Slightly; 3 Moderately; 4 Very; 5 Extremely.

## Researcher answer key — keep off the participant page

Map distance is converted to real distance by multiplying the number of centimetres by 2 kilometres per centimetre. The application answer is 6 km, since 3 × 2 = 6. The claim is incorrect for this map; the scale, rather than a one-to-one assumption, determines the conversion.

Accept equivalent units and a correct diagram or arithmetic explanation. Correct misunderstandings after the practice, without previewing the experimental topics. The practice confidence question demonstrates the 1–5 self-report scale; practice reflection evaluation uses 0–2.

Practice reflection examples: **0** “ok”; **1** “It tells you the distance”; **2** “Each centimetre on this map stands for two kilometres, so a four-centimetre route is eight kilometres.” 

Finish once the participant can operate the interface. If they cannot, resolve the interface issue before starting an experimental round.
